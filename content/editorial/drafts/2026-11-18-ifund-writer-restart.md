## 2. Blog / helpful fundraising guide

**Title:** Six small changes that can make your fundraiser easier to support

**Summary:** Your story doesn't need to be perfect. Make it clear, honest and easy to share.

**Body:**

Fundraising is personal. The strongest version of your campaign isn't necessarily the longest or most dramatic. It's the one people can understand and trust.

**Start with a specific goal.** Explain what you're trying to accomplish and the needs that donations would cover. If you aren't sure about an expense, say it's an estimate. Clarity helps people understand why support matters.

**Let your real voice lead.** Share what happened and why the fundraiser matters to you. Avoid pressure, exaggerated urgency or details about others that you don't have permission to publish.

**Choose an image that tells the story.** A clear, relevant photo can help people recognize what the campaign is about. If you're using a photo of someone else, make sure they agree to that use.

**Make it easy to share.** Write a short introduction a supporter can use, and make sure the fundraiser link works. Not everyone can donate, but some people may be comfortable passing your story along.

**Keep your supporters informed.** When something changes, write a brief update about the progress, the remaining needs and any verified milestones. You don't need to wait for a dramatic announcement.

**Thank people without making assumptions.** Appreciate gifts, encouragement and shares in a way that respects people's privacy and choices.

These are ways to improve the quality and reach of your presentation, not guarantees of donations. IFund's developing guide is intended to keep these simple decisions within reach of everyday organizers.
